Screening Protocol

A structured workflow for organizing community diabetes awareness and screening activities.

How Does the Screening Work?

Each participant moves through six organized stages.

01

Registration and Consent

Register the participant, explain the screening process and obtain informed consent.

Estimated time: 2 minutes
02

Risk Questionnaire

Ask the seven educational risk-awareness questions, including family history and physical activity.

Estimated time: 3 minutes
03

Waist Measurement

Record waist circumference using a measuring tape and appropriate measurement guidance.

Estimated time: 2 minutes
04

Urine Glucose Screening

If included in the screening programme, a trained person follows the test-strip manufacturer's instructions and records the result.

Estimated time: 3–5 minutes
05

Assessment and Record Keeping

Review the questionnaire and recorded observations. Explain that the screening findings are not a diagnosis.

Estimated time: 2 minutes
06

Counselling and Referral

Provide general awareness information and direct participants with concerns to a qualified healthcare professional for appropriate assessment.

Estimated time: 2–3 minutes

Estimated Time Per Participant

15 Minutes

Approximate time for one participant, excluding waiting time.

The estimated workflow takes approximately 14–17 minutes depending on the participant and testing process.

Important Safety Principles

Informed Consent

Participation should be voluntary, with the process explained clearly.

Privacy

Personal information and screening records should be handled confidentially.

Hygiene

Use appropriate protective equipment and follow safe collection and disposal procedures.

Professional Referral

Abnormal screening results or health concerns require appropriate professional follow-up.

Important Medical Disclaimer

Urine glucose strips are not a replacement for blood glucose testing or HbA1c testing. A negative urine result cannot rule out diabetes, and a positive result does not confirm it. Screening should be conducted under appropriate healthcare supervision.
