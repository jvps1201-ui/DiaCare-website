KNOWLEDGE IS THE FIRST STEP

Understanding Diabetes

Learn about diabetes, its types, common symptoms and the importance of early screening.

01 / INTRODUCTION

What Is Diabetes?

Diabetes mellitus is a chronic health condition that occurs when the body cannot produce enough insulin or cannot use insulin effectively.

Insulin is a hormone produced by the pancreas. It helps glucose move from the bloodstream into the body's cells, where it is used for energy.

When insulin does not function properly, glucose can build up in the blood, resulting in high blood sugar levels.

02 / TYPES

Different Types of Diabetes

Diabetes can occur in different forms, each with its own causes and characteristics.

01

Type 1 Diabetes

An autoimmune condition in which the body's immune system attacks insulin-producing cells in the pancreas.

Often develops in childhood or adolescence
02

Type 2 Diabetes

Occurs when the body becomes resistant to insulin or does not produce enough insulin to maintain normal blood glucose.

The most common type
03

Gestational Diabetes

A type of diabetes that develops during pregnancy and requires appropriate medical monitoring.

Occurs during pregnancy

03 / AWARENESS

Common Symptoms

Some people with diabetes may not experience noticeable symptoms, making screening important.

💧

Frequent Urination

Passing urine more frequently than usual.

🥤

Increased Thirst

Feeling unusually thirsty.

⚡

Fatigue

Feeling unusually tired or weak.

👁️

Blurred Vision

Experiencing temporary changes in vision.

⚖️

Unexplained Weight Changes

Changes in body weight without an obvious reason.

🩹

Slow Healing

Some wounds may take longer to heal.

Important: Symptoms alone cannot confirm or rule out diabetes. Consult a qualified healthcare professional for appropriate testing.

04 / SCREENING TECHNOLOGY

Understanding Urine Glucose Strips

A preliminary screening indicator used in our proposed Community Diabetes Screening Kit.

How Do They Work?

Urine glucose strips contain a chemical testing pad that changes colour when glucose is detected in urine.

The resulting colour is compared with the manufacturer's colour chart to interpret the approximate glucose level.

Advantages

  • Relatively inexpensive.
  • Non-invasive testing method.
  • Easy to transport and store according to instructions.
  • Can provide a preliminary indication of glucose in urine.

Limitations

  • Glucose may not appear in urine until blood glucose becomes sufficiently high.
  • A negative result cannot rule out diabetes.
  • Results can be influenced by kidney function and other factors.
  • It cannot replace blood glucose testing or HbA1c.

05 / TEST ACCURACY

Sensitivity and Specificity

These two concepts help explain how accurately screening tests identify possible cases.

Sensitivity

The ability of a test to correctly identify people who actually have a disease.

High sensitivity = fewer false negatives.

Specificity

The ability of a test to correctly identify people who do not have a disease.

High specificity = fewer false positives.

False Positive

A screening result indicates a possible disease even though the person does not have that disease.

May cause unnecessary concern and further testing.

False Negative

A screening result appears negative even though the person actually has the disease.

May delay diagnosis and medical care.

Which Error Is More Concerning?

In community diabetes screening, false negatives are particularly concerning because people with diabetes may remain unidentified and miss opportunities for timely medical evaluation.

However, false positives also matter because they can cause unnecessary anxiety and follow-up. Both errors should be minimized through appropriate screening and confirmatory testing.
